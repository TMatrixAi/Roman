I want you to perform a complete statistical audit of my Tennis Stats Engine using my entire available prediction history (not a sample — every prediction record on file).

DO NOT modify any code.

DO NOT make any changes.

This is an audit only.

Your job is to identify anything that is statistically incorrect, misleading, inconsistent, poorly calibrated, overconfident, underconfident, unnecessary, redundant, or potentially harming prediction accuracy.

Treat this like a professional quantitative model validation.

Before starting, confirm and report:

- Total number of prediction records available and the date range they span.
- Whether this evaluation is a true walk-forward/out-of-time validation (each prediction using only data available before that match) or something else — state explicitly which one it is, and flag if any data leakage is possible.
- Whether the dataset spans the Task #68/#75 Data Quality recalibration change. If so, report calibration and all downstream metrics separately for the pre-change period, the post-change period, and combined, since blending both regimes could distort findings.

Inspect every available prediction and analyze:

- Final prediction
- Actual winner
- Win probability
- Actual result
- Raw ensemble probability
- Calibrated probability
- Data Quality
- Upset Risk
- Model Agreement
- Matchup Closeness
- Recommendation
- Elite Tier
- Segment Specialist
- Monte Carlo Simulator
- Every individual model vote
- Surface Elo
- Serve & Return
- Recent Form
- Fatigue
- Availability
- Head-to-Head
- Match Load Recovery
- Weather (if used)

For every issue you report, include statistical evidence, including a 95% confidence interval (via bootstrap or equivalent) for every headline metric (Brier Score, log loss, ECE, calibration-in-the-large, subgroup accuracy).

Do NOT speculate.

Do NOT recommend changes unless the historical evidence supports them.

For any subgroup or bucket (e.g., Elite tier, a specific model, a recommendation tier, a DQ band) with fewer than 100 observations, explicitly flag that finding as statistically unstable due to small sample size, and state the actual n for that bucket.

When testing many thresholds or models at once, note where an apparent pattern could plausibly be due to chance given the number of comparisons made, rather than a real effect.
