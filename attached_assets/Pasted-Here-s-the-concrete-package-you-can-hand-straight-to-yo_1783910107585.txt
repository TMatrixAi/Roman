## Step 2: Add a real calibration layer

This is the fix for your 76.6%→62.6% and 88.7%→76.5% gaps. Don't hand-tune the confidence formula — fit a proper calibrator on held-out data. sklearn's `IsotonicRegression` is the standard tool for this exact problem:[1][2]

```python
from sklearn.isotonic import IsotonicRegression
from sklearn.calibration import calibration_curve

# raw_probs, outcomes = your model's OOS predictions and actual results
prob_true, prob_pred = calibration_curve(outcomes, raw_probs, n_bins=10)

iso = IsotonicRegression(out_of_bounds='clip')
iso.fit(prob_pred, prob_true)   # fit on binned reliability curve, not raw points

calibrated_prob = iso.predict(raw_probs)  # apply to future predictions
```

Important details specific to your setup:

- Fit the isotonic curve against the binned reliability curve (prob_pred/prob_true), not raw individual predictions — fitting directly on 0/1 outcomes tends to produce a jagged, overfit curve, especially with your ~4,000-sample OOS set.[3]
- sklearn's own guidance is that isotonic needs a decent sample size (roughly 1,000+) to avoid overfitting; with 4,081 out-of-sample predictions you're right at the edge, so also compute Platt/sigmoid scaling as a fallback and use whichever generalizes better on a fresh holdout.[2]
- Refit the calibrator periodically (e.g., every few hundred new graded matches) since your model's confidence distribution will shift as you remove Availability and reweight things.
- Track Expected Calibration Error (ECE) before/after — under 0.03 is well-calibrated, above 0.05 means bet sizing off your confidence numbers is still misleading:[4][5]

```python
def compute_ece(y_true, y_prob, n_bins=10):
    bins = np.linspace(0, 1, n_bins + 1)
    ece = 0
    for lo, hi in zip(bins[:-1], bins[1:]):
        mask = (y_prob > lo) & (y_prob <= hi)
        if mask.sum() > 0:
            acc = y_true[mask].mean()
            conf = y_prob[mask].mean()
            ece += (mask.sum() / len(y_true)) * abs(acc - conf)
    return ece
```
